# Traveller profile

Captured from the buyer 2026-08-12. This is the file that makes this purchase
different from a generic "best monitor under $200" answer, and it should be read
before any recommendation in `research/` is trusted.

## Usage pattern

| | |
|---|---|
| Locations where work productivity matters | **One, possibly two** |
| Primary | This US property (Connecticut) — in-laws' residence |
| Frequency | Roughly **once a year** |
| Duration | About **three weeks** per visit |
| Possible second | A family member in Europe. Not yet a commitment |
| Everything else | Shorter trips, leisure-weighted, productivity not a concern |

Working days per year at the destination: call it **~15**. That number does more
work in the analysis than any spec on any datasheet — see
[`research/value-analysis.md`](../research/value-analysis.md).

The important structural point: these are **family visits of multi-week duration**,
not a nomadic pattern. There is no circuit of a dozen cities to equip. "Depositing
monitors around the world" is not the plan and would not make sense; leaving one
screen at the one place visited annually for three weeks is a single purchase, not
a strategy. The buyer raised this objection themselves and it resolves this way.

## Anecdotes and prior experience — why portable monitors are disfavoured

These are first-hand and they carry weight. Recorded here because the reasoning is
not obvious from specs, and because a future agent reading only the spec sheet
would recommend exactly the wrong thing.

1. **Has owned several portable monitors over the years. Found them
   underwhelming.** Not a spec complaint — a satisfaction complaint, repeated
   across multiple units.
2. **They do not fit carry-on well.** A 15.6″ panel plus its case eats a
   meaningful share of a cabin bag, competing with things that are not optional.
3. **Carry-on brings anxiety, checked luggage brings damage.** In checked bags the
   worry is constant and, in this buyer's experience, justified.
4. **A 15.6″ monitor packed in checked luggage, wrapped in bubble wrap and padded
   with clothing, arrived broken.** One trial, one failure, total loss. First-time
   failure of a protective strategy that most people would consider adequate.
5. **A hard case fixes the breakage but not the economics.** Once a rigid case is
   added on top of the monitor, the combination consumes enough luggage volume
   that the whole exercise stops being worth it.

**What these anecdotes are actually evidence about:** they are an argument against
*transporting* a monitor. They are not an argument about *screen size*, and the two
should not be conflated. A screen bought at the destination and left there is never
exposed to any of the five failure modes above — which means the transport
experience, properly applied, argues for **buying locally and abandoning in place**,
and says nothing at all about whether that local purchase should be 15.6″ or 27″.

## Consequences for the decision

- **Portability has zero value** and should not be paid for.
- **Transit fragility is irrelevant** — the panel makes one car journey from a shop.
- **Abandonment is the exit strategy.** If visits stop, whatever is bought is
  stranded in Connecticut. That is a real argument for the cheap end of the range
  and against a premium panel.
- **The second location, if it happens, gets its own local purchase.** Buying one
  screen and shuttling it between Connecticut and Europe reproduces every failure
  mode above, at higher cost than simply buying a second cheap monitor there.

## Note on scope

This profile is about the buyer, not about this monitor, and it will be relevant to
any future "should I buy this at home or at the destination" question. If a second
such decision comes up it probably deserves a home outside this repo rather than
being copied — flagging rather than acting on that now.
